02

Nine Nights · Nine Forms

नवदुर्गा — Navadurga

The nine names come in this exact order from the Devi Kavacham, recited before the Durga Saptashati — Shailaputri, Brahmacharini, Chandraghanta, Kushmanda, Skandamata, Katyayani, Kalaratri, Mahagauri, Siddhidatri. Together they are one life of the Mother: birth on the mountain, penance, the wedding, the making of the cosmos, motherhood, war, the dark night, the return of light, and fulfilment. Each card carries her vahana, her bhog, her chakra, her mantra and her dhyana shloka.

03

Ten Lenses of One Light

दश महाविद्या — The Ten Great Wisdoms

In Shakta Tantra the Mahavidyas are not ten goddesses but ten ways the one Shakti shows herself — from Kali, who devours time, to Kamala, who pours abundance. The framework insists that the auspicious and the terrible are both her face: liberation comes not by rejecting the dark, but by seeing the Mother inside it. The Mundamala Tantra gives the ten-name order; the Mahabhagavata Purana tells how Sati took these ten forms to surround Shiva in the ten directions. Each card gives her mool mantra as recorded in the Tantric recensions.

Practitioner's caution These are intense practices. Traditional Shakta Tantra holds that the fierce Mahavidyas — especially Kali, Chinnamasta, Dhumavati and Bagalamukhi — are taken up only after diksha from a qualified guru, and never for harming another. What is offered here is for reading, remembrance and devotion, not for initiating yourself into a sadhana.
04

Where the Earth Became a Temple

शक्तिपीठ — The Shakti Peethas

When Sati gave up her body in Daksha's fire, Shiva carried her and the pieces fell across the land — each place a seat of the Mother. Every count belongs to a different text: four Adi Peethas to devotional tradition, eighteen to Adi Shankaracharya's Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram, fifty-one to the Pithanirnaya of the Tantra-chudamani, and one hundred eight to the Devi Bhagavata Purana. Open any list below.

The eighteen of the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram, in the order of the verses. This is the most widely recited fixed sequence, and the stotra itself is in the Mantra Sangrah below.

The core textual list. The Sanskrit of the Tantra-chudamani / Pithanirnaya says pañcāśad-eka-pīṭhāni — "fifty-one Peethas". Names follow the standard recension order.

The fifty-one of the Pithanirnaya with the manuscript expansion to fifty-two. Shakti, Bhairava and place are given where the tradition records them; where traditions differ, the card says so rather than inventing certainty.

From the Devi Bhagavata Purana, Skandha 7, Adhyaya 30 — the Devipitha-varnanam. Here each seat is paired with a name of the Goddess; several are cosmic or symbolic locations rather than standing temples.

The 18, 51 and 108 enumerations are the most securely attested name-by-name lists. The others are traditional but vary by recension, region and commentator — which is why this portal shows them as traditions, not as one settled number. Modern "52 temple" lists that add Ambaji, Danteshwari, Saptashrungi or Chintpurni are living shrines, but they are not part of the original Pithanirnaya sequence.

05

Digital Darshan · Living Bhakti

वर्चुअल मंदिर — The Virtual Mandir

Light the diya, ring the bell, offer pushpa, change the darshan, count a mala of 108, and leave your sankalp. This is a devotional aid made of light and sound — a place to sit for two minutes, not a replacement for vidhi, a priest, or a temple you can walk into.

06

Sound as the Body of the Goddess

मंत्र संग्रह — The Mantra Sangrah

Mantras are held to be the sonic body of each form — not sentences to be understood but sounds to be sat with. Everything here is given in Devanagari with a plain transliteration: beej and mool mantras, the nine Navadurga, the ten Mahavidyas, the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram, Devi Suktam, Aigiri Nandini, the aartis and the chalisa.

07

Kripa Kathayein · Stories of Her Grace

कृपा कथाएँ — Where the Mother Showed Herself

Every Peetha carries a sthala-purana, and every generation adds its own account of a prayer answered. What follows are the traditional kathas and the well-recorded devotional accounts — told as the tradition tells them. They are offered as faith and story, not as claims that can be tested; the Mother's grace is known in the life of the devotee, not in a laboratory.

08

Grahan · What the Mother Asks of You

धर्म — The Teaching to Carry Home

Darshan ends in conduct. After the mantra and the katha comes the one thing the Mother actually asks: that you hold dharma. Not as a banner to raise against others, but as a line you do not cross yourself.

धर्म एव हतो हन्ति धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः।
तस्माद्धर्मो न हन्तव्यो मा धर्मो हतोऽवधीत्॥

dharmo eva hato hanti dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ |
tasmād dharmo na hantavyo mā dharmo hato'vadhīt ||

Dharma, when destroyed, destroys; dharma, when protected, protects. Therefore dharma must never be destroyed — lest dharma, destroyed, destroy us.

Mahabharata · Vana Parva · also cited in Manusmriti 8.15

Your sankalp — the last word

Take one thing from this portal into tomorrow: a name of the Mother you will say before you sleep, one act of ahimsa you will not compromise, one person you will protect who cannot protect themselves. That is how dharma is kept — rakshitaḥ, by being protected, one small act at a time. ॐ सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः। जय माता दी॥

Built as a single, self-contained page: eight darshanas, one Mother. Everything here is offered for study and devotion in the Sanatana Shakta parampara.

Sources for the lists: the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram (traditionally attributed to Adi Shankaracharya) for the eighteen; the Pithanirnaya of the Tantra-chudamani for the fifty-one and its manuscript expansion to fifty-two; the Devi Bhagavata Purana (7.30) for the hundred and eight; the Devi Kavacham for the Navadurga names; the Mundamala Tantra and Todala Tantra for the Mahavidya sequence.
